# Surface brief: Telephone dashboard

## Mode
Operate. Built code-led: there is no comp of this screen.

## Audience and job
Someone who has just cloned the repository and started `telephone dashboard`. They know nothing
about the project. Jobs, in order: understand what the project does, start an experiment without
a terminal, watch it run, and read the result in plain words. Bardia also puts it on a projector
for tutorials.

## Constraints
- Only the author's photographs and pipeline outputs appear as images.
- Charts are hand-written SVG.
- A paid run shows its estimated cost and needs a confirmation. Simulations are labelled.
- Motion states change and nothing else; `prefers-reduced-motion` is honoured.

## Direction contract

THESIS: The screen is a neutral viewing surface for judging a photograph against its own copies,
with the measuring scale on the page rather than hidden in a number. It refuses the grey-app
default of stat cards with one accent, and it refuses a darkroom or terminal costume.

OWN-WORLD: The photographer's calibration target (grey card, step wedge, colour chart). Neutral
light-grey ground with no colour cast; white work surfaces; near-black ink. Colour appears only as
flat square patches that each mean one thing: blue Platform, orange Reader, green Painter, and the
reserved status colours. System sans throughout, tabular figures in columns. Square corners on
patches and photographs, small radii on controls.

STORY: The visitor learns that a photo goes round a loop, starts a free run, sees each generation
arrive, then reads where the picture landed between "same picture" and "different picture".

FIRST VIEWPORT: Left rail with the project name, five destinations and the budget. Main column:
one sentence saying what Telephone is, the next action as the single primary button, the loop
drawn as three labelled patches, and the six photographs with their latest result.

FORM: Calibration target, first of seven on my list. seed: none (unavailable). Chosen by Bardia on
10 November 2026 through the question tool.

SIGNATURE: The step wedge. Drift is drawn as a marker on a strip of grey steps running from the
original to a different photograph, wherever a drift number appears.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the
verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
